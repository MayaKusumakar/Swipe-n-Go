## Interview 1
**Interviewer:** Noah W
**Note Taker:** Evelyn Xiao
**Interviewee:** Lucas G
**Date:** 11/16/2026
**Recording:** [Recording](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1FBJkSbrkBDrKI_gMIL3SYW_z52Ee03sd/view?usp=drive_link)


**1. Their answer to the 5-second test**
- I’m assuming this is a “meeting friends” app. Based on the wide range of example events+interest fields, it appears to be broad. Since there was no barrier to entry, it appears that this is open to anybody, not just University students.

**2. Where they got stuck during the task**
- Did not get stuck when creating an event
Did not get stuck when signing up for an event
Did not get stuck when deleting an event.

**3.Their reaction to your AI feature**
- The event page seemed like a lot of information to take in at first glance. After taking some time, it mostly made sense. 
Expanded that the order the information was presented in didn’t quite match the order he would expect or prioritize. He provided an example where he would rather have ABOUT appear first instead of DETAILS because when he is clicking onto an event he cares more about what it is. “First, I’ll check if I’m interested in it then check when it’s happening”.

**4.Their answers to the debrief questions**
- Yes, the interviewee still believed that this was a broad “meeting friends” app that is open to anybody, not just University students.
I think that everybody uses Instagram nowadays, so if you wanted to release this I might look for an ability to link your Google account to the app

**5.Your post-interview synthesis**
- The version of the app tested lacks ucdavis email verification, so the interviewee came to the conclusion that the app was intended for anyone
The core loop appeared to be intuitive for the interviewee; moreover, they seemed to easily navigate through each of the tasks provided to them. It may be worth increasing the complexity of these tasks or preparing additional materials for them to fully engage with the apps capabilities
Potentially bringing a flyer for them to scan to test the flyer scanning option, or telling them to come up with a short description of the app to test the text parsing feature
When asked about what the app might be missing, the interviewee mentioned the ability to link other existing accounts like Google or Instagram to the app. This comment emphasizes that the status quo already provides strong solutions to the problem we are trying to solve. This may imply that, for the Guest side of the interaction, we need to continue to build this app as a service that complements existing social media accounts rather than replacing them outright.



## Interview 2
**Interviewer:** Noah W
**Note Taker:** Evelyn Xiao
**Interviewee:** Hardik S
**Date:** 11/16/2026
**Recording:** [Recording](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1bOvZd76hKQ_N4MpIiZYuRQdLnszYYuLt/view?usp=drive_link)


**1. Their answer to the 5-second test**
- I think this app is designated to be something that lets you explore local events based on your personal interests. Something that stands out to me are the infographics and animated visuals. This gives the app an extra level of polish
As someone who is more tech savvy, it was very easy for me to get accustomed to what the app is trying to convey. The interviewee provides the example of the presented layout that explains swiping one way to sign up for an event and the other to pass on the event.
Interviewee comments that he believes the app might not be as clear for users who are less tech savvy but want to get involved with local events. Proposes that a disclaimer explaining what the swiping means would be very beneficial for these potential users

**2. Where they got stuck during the task**
- When clicking on specific events/tabs from particular screens, the app would crash unexpectedly. Reopenning the app brought the user back to where they left off.
After completing the first task, the interviewee began engaging with the placeholder group chat feature and commented that the bots were very proactive.
There was a couple of seconds of uncertainty when trying to find details on the event from the group chat page; however, the user quickly determined that the ‘i’ button in the top right corner was where to proceed. The interviewee immediately found the event details from there.
The user quickly found the correct section to start creating the event post; however, they completely ignored the flyer scanning feature. The user proceeded to enter the information manually based on what the example flyer said. After completing the task, entering the details through the bottom navigation bar once again caused the app to unexpectedly crash. Reopenning the app took the user back to the home page without losing any previous work.

**3.Their reaction to your AI feature**
- In terms of friction, I would say that the 30 person limit was a bit restrictive. I would like the ability to go above this limit for larger events like concerts or movie watch parties. Having a no limit feature or expanding the feature a bit more would be nice.

**4.Their answers to the debrief questions**
- If I had to change one thing in particular, I would say that when you go into an event group chat (Plans > Group Chat) it isn’t exactly clear where to get the information for the event. When I see the ‘i’ icon intended to get you this information, my first assumption is that it is supposed to provide information about the app rather than about the event itself. Something that says ‘Event Info’ rather than the ‘i’ icon would be beneficial, because I normally associate the ‘i’ with privacy policies.
The interviewee stated that they are a disorganized planner and spontaneous person when it comes to making plans. He expanded, stating that the app would be better suited to people who are more organized and keen on planning things out with a specific schedule. For that reason, this kind of app would be better suited for that demographic and he doesn’t think that he would use the app himself.
More often than not, if my friends are around in-person I just ask them if they want to go somewhere. On occasion, I’ll ask if people are available over weekends; however, typically I’ll just ask them on the spot. If I do want to plan something out, I’ll typically write a more detailed text message. 
If other friends aren’t interested in joining, I typically go by myself.
I would say that I am able to have a good time at events even when I go by myself. The interviewee provided the example of going to the Davis Fashion Club alone since his friends aren’t particularly interested in fashion. The interviewee determined that he should go to the event themself to explore, and happened to make good friends there. He says that he is an introvert, but he doesn’t have issues with socializing once at an event. He goes on to add that he could see this being more of a challenge for people that are a little anxious socially.
If I get along well with people I met at those events I normally swap contact information with them. However, I do not typically bring up the idea of going to another event or engaging in another activity with people unless I really get to know them. While I get on a friendly-basis with people, engaging in friendly activities requires more interaction than solely through something that happens spontaneously. If I am already friends with someone I am more comfortable with making spontaneous plans; however, there have been cases where I just met someone and invited them to get a coffee together.
Typically, yes. There needs to be history and trust when making plans on top of the mutual interests and the person seeming friendly.

**5.Your post-interview synthesis**
- This version’s initial impressions and core loop seem to be effective based on these results; moreover, the interview provides some notable insights into potential changes and the specific target audience. Aside from some crashes when navigating to pages from specific screens, the user had little to no issues when engaging with the app and its features. Some minor changes that can be implemented based on these conversations are changing the ‘i’ icon in the group chat screen to better match user expectations and to provide more freedom to enter the meeting attendee limit when creating an event. A part of the interview that grabbed my attention was the user’s tendency to completely ignore the scan flyer feature. This could be a result of feeling more comfortable with entering the details manually, or could just be the actual functionality not being a large enough button to catch the user’s attention. Some comments were made about making sure the app is approachable for less tech savvy users; however, more testing will be needed before we start implementing disclaimer popups or tutorials especially given our target audience of college students. My major takeaways from this were making the aforementioned changes to allow user actions to feel intuitive and uninhibited, better emphasizing the flyer scanning feature to encourage users to engage with it, and ensuring that the app is targeted towards students (guests and hosts) who want to address the core problems of A) College event visibility/accessibility and B) Making it easier to find other relevant students to attend/engage with campus events. 

## Interview 3
**Interviewer:** Haylie Tan
**Note Taker:** Noah Wong
**Interviewee:** Joey Mai
**Date:** 11/17/2026
**Recording:** [Recording](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qobMDicHp8xDUXekl4Rl1ssxSxMn5IlF/view?usp=drive_link)


**1. Their answer to the 5-second test**
- “Take a look at this screen for a few seconds. Without clicking anything yet... what do you think this app does?” It seems like its a sport club app. Talking about a 5k run
- The interviewee’s immediate impression was that the app was specifically designed for a sports club event. Their attention appeared to be focused on one particular event, so the provided description seems to have been regarding that. While not completely accurate to the broad scope of the app, the keywords chosen indicate that the general uses of the app were implied by the layout and interface.

**2. Where they got stuck during the task**
- Imagine you want to look through the events on this app. How would you go about it? I would go on the discover page. I would swipe through the different filter options. 
- Similar note: if you wanted to find an event locally based off location, how would you go about it? I would go to the explore page and search where I am at and what is nearby.
- Say you wanted to connect to others at an event you already saved. How do you save the event and find others who are also going? It seems like if I click on the event, it shows who else is going.
- When asked how they would use the app to look for events on campus, the interviewee explained their process of navigating to the ‘Discover’ tab and exploring listed options from there. 
When asked to look for events that were close to the user, the interviewee went to the ‘Explore’ screen and the ‘Search Nearby’ features without issue.
Given the task of connecting with other people going to an already saved event, the interviewee began the process by attempting to save the event. After some consideration, they ended up finding the list of attendees by going to the event page directly. While this wasn’t the most direct way of completing the task, we predict that this  was the chosen method since it uses portions of the app that the user had already been to. 

**3.Their reaction to your AI feature**
- The user was able to parse the different aspects of the app's functionalities with minimal difficulties. While there was some confusion with how to engage with the Swiping feature and some unorthodox routes to finding other attendees, the user's reactions to the features themselves were postive overall. They easily located and utilized the event summaries in order to understand saved events when prompted to in the task section; moreover, the AI information provided to them was interpreted with no observable friction. It seems that the individual features of the app were intuitive enough for the testing experience; however, more interviews should be able to shed more light on whether the core loop stand out enough.
  
**4.Their answers to the debrief questions**
- On a scale of 1 to 5, how difficult was that? 2; pretty easy tasks
- Was there any point where you weren’t sure what to do next? Swiping option or arrows to show that it is a swipe-based homescreen
- What is one thing you would change to make this easier? Description on how to use the app
- Do you see yourself using this app? If it is UC Davis exclusive, yes
- The interviewee stated that the tasks provided were pretty easy. They went on to rate the difficulty of using the app at a 2 out of 5, where 1 is least difficult and 5 is the most difficult.
- When asked if there was a point where the interviewee didn’t know what to do next, they explained that they didn't immediately recognize the swiping interaction. They compared the action to Tinder and suggested that the app have visuals that illustrate the intended swiping interface. Explained that the functionality could benefit from something that indicates that it’s a swiping thing.
- When the interviewee was asked what change they would make to make using the app easier they responded that a little more description on how to use the app when first engaging with it would be helpful. During their explanation they referenced their difficulties with recognizing what elements required swiping. 
- As a concluding comment, the user stated that the app seemed useful if it were for UC Davis specifically. However, they communicated that they wouldn’t use the app outside of that context.


**5.Your post-interview synthesis**
- When focusing on the Guest side of the user experience, the observations gleaned from this interview were positive overall. The responses all seemed to indicate that the app's overall purpose and page breakdown made intuitive sense for our target audience of UC Davis college students. Some points that might be worth further investigating include the immediate understandability of the titular swiping feature, the ease of navigation between different screens, and the reason why this app appeared useful for UC Davis events but not overall. Luckily, the interviewee's success with the core loop indicates that accomplishing abstract tasks within the current iteration makes logical sense. However, working to smooth out the minor points of friction we have documented and experimenting with how to make the main pull of the app more impactful will be worthwhile efforts as we proceed through these Milestones.
